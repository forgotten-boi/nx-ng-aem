Waypoints & directions

    In an emergency

    Call 999 or 112, ask for the Police, then Mountain Rescue.

    Your grid reference: no GPS fix yet

    No signal for a call? A text to 999 may get through — but only if you have registered with emergencySMS first: text register to 999 and follow the reply.

    Before you go

    • Check the mountain forecast: Met Office (Peak District) and MWIS. The plateau is far wilder than the valley, and cloud can come down quickly.
    • Carry OS Explorer OL1 (Dark Peak) and a compass, and know how to use them — phone batteries fade fast in the cold.
    • Waterproofs, warm layers, hat and gloves, food, water, a head torch and a charged phone (plus a power bank).
    • Tell someone your route and when you expect to be back. Allow 4–5 hours including stops.

    On the hill

    • The top of Grindsbrook Clough is a scramble up a rocky stream bed: slippery when wet and dangerous when the brook is in spate. In winter it can need winter skills and kit.
    • The edge path is clear, but the plateau interior — including the true summit — is a maze of peat groughs where it is easy to get lost in mist.
    • Stay on the paths to protect the restored moorland. Dogs must be on a short lead on access land from 1 March to 31 July, and near livestock at any time.
    • Moorland fires spread fast: no barbecues or fires. Report any fire on 999.

    Getting there

    • Train: Edale station is on the Hope Valley line between Manchester Piccadilly and Sheffield.
    • Car: pay and display car park in Edale beside the station; it fills early at weekends.
    • Pubs and a café in Edale — nothing on the hill.

    About this app

    • The route line is compiled from published grid references of landmarks; points between them, distances and most heights are approximate. Use it alongside a map, not instead of one.
    • Tracking uses your phone’s GPS. Your position and track never leave this device unless you share or download them.
